Your questions, answered

The things a 50-year, locally-owned group rightly asks before handing over the marketing.

Will we lose our local followers if you merge the Instagrams into one account?

No. We consolidate, we don't delete. The main brand account becomes the home, the store accounts are migrated or kept as local handles that feed into it, and content stays locally relevant for each market. You keep your audiences; they just stop being thirteen disconnected silos and start reinforcing one brand. Nothing is thrown away.

Do we keep our in-house marketing team?

Yes, and we want you to. They keep shooting and posting, exactly as today. We are the central brain on top of them: the strategy, the scripts, the design, the ads, the SEO and the measurement that make thirteen separate efforts add up to one brand. We make your team's work go further, we don't replace it.

How does vendor co-op lower what we actually spend on ads?

Manufacturers like Ashley and the appliance and mattress brands set aside co-op advertising dollars when their product and branding are featured to spec. Most retailers leave that money on the table. We build the program co-op compliant from day one, file the claims, and report what's claimed, so a real share of the media is reimbursed and your out-of-pocket on ads is lower than the headline budget.

Is our data, and our customers' data, safe?

Yes, accurately rather than fearfully. Your checkout already runs on Shopify, which is certified PCI-DSS Level 1, the same standard banks require, and it does not store card numbers on your servers. The new build meets that same bar by design: HTTPS everywhere, a firewall and CDN in front of the site, dashboards behind secure sign-in, least-privilege access, and automatic backups. We market security as a customer trust signal, we never overstate the risk.

Can we start small? What if it doesn't work?

That's exactly how we recommend starting. A 2-3 store pilot, fully managed from about $5,000/mo, so you see it working in your own point-of-sale numbers before committing across the group. It's phased so you only pay for what's switched on, with a minimum initial term and then month to month. You prove it on your own dashboard, then we roll the proven system across all 13.

What stays local, and what becomes central?

Central: one brand, one content bank, one promo calendar, one paid engine, one dashboard system, one point of contact. Local: each store stays locally owned, keeps its own identity, its own local content and promos, its own customers and its own numbers. We centralize the engine, not the ownership.

How fast do we get our deliverables?

Fast. Written content is ready for you to review within a day or two of the onboarding call. Drafts of every deliverable, brand, website, profiles and content, are ready inside the first week. After a 3-4 day round of edits, the whole build is signed off and live in about two weeks. Google profile verification and the live per-store inventory feed start in that window and finish as those external steps clear.

Who do we actually talk to?

One person: Andrei, your single point of contact, who owns the relationship, the calendar and the results. Behind him is a named team you'll know, Kabrin on the AI systems, Sky on the content, and Nate's sales advisory on the higher tiers. No rotating cast, no coordination tax.

What about the bad reviews, and what AI says about us?

Two parts. First, most of the worst reviews online belong to an unrelated US company you share a name with, and we separate your brand from theirs so it stops bleeding onto you. Second, the real, fixable theme is appliance after-sale service. We help you own the warranty hand-off, respond to the specific reviews, and build a steady flow of fresh positive reviews, which is what shifts what the AI engines say over time. We monitor it and report the movement each quarter.

How do you report, and how often?

Everything in your point-of-sale dollars, on a per-store dashboard with a group roll-up (the layout above). Weekly numbers, a monthly review, and quarterly planning tied to the promo calendar, with vendor co-op claimed shown separately. You always know what every dollar is doing, by store.
